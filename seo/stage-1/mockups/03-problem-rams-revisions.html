How to manage RAMS revisions and prove who acknowledged the current version

What a defensible process looks like when a method statement changes mid-job: who approves the revision, how the superseded version is withdrawn, and what record shows each operative was briefed on the version they are working to.

1. Why revisions go wrong

Most RAMS problems are not about the first version. They begin when the sequence of work changes: a different access method, a new subcontractor, a hazard that was not on the original assessment. The method statement is updated, usually quickly, and three things fail in sequence.

  • The revision is accepted informally, so there is no record of who judged it adequate.
  • The old version stays on site, in a folder or on a phone, and the crew keeps working to it.
  • The re-briefing is done verbally, so nobody can later show who was told what and when.
Under CDM 2015 the contractor must plan, manage and monitor the work so it is carried out without risk, and must provide appropriate information and instruction to workers (regulations 15 and 14). HSG65 describes "Do" as implementing the plan and "Check" as measuring that controls are in place. A revision with no approval or briefing record fails both.

2. Approve, supersede, re-brief

A defensible revision process has three recorded steps, in this order.

Approve the revision

A competent person reviews the changed method and the linked risk assessments. Comments that do not change the stage are fine. A rejection or a request for changes carries a reason. The approver and the time are recorded.

Supersede the previous version

The old revision is marked superseded, not deleted. Anything that linked to it, a permit, a RAMS pack, a toolbox talk, should raise a warning so it can be checked against the new version.

Re-brief and record acknowledgement

Every operative who will work to the revised method is briefed on what changed and acknowledges the specific revision. The acknowledgement is tied to the revision identifier, not to the document title.

3. What the acknowledgement record must show

FieldWhy it matters at audit
Method statement reference and revisionProves which version was briefed, not just that "RAMS were signed".
Person acknowledging, with name snapshotSurvives staff changes and account removals.
Acknowledgement type and timeShows whether it was a briefing, a read-and-sign, or a re-brief after change.
Who approved the revision, and whenLinks the briefing to a competent decision.
Status of the previous revisionShows the old version was withdrawn.

4. Checklist: before the crew starts on a revised method

  • Revision approved by a named competent person, with the reason for change recorded
  • Previous revision marked superseded; linked permits and packs checked
  • Every operative on the task acknowledged the new revision
  • Toolbox talk or briefing session recorded with attendance
  • Permit (if any) re-validated against the new method
